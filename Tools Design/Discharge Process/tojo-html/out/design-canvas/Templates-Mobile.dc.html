Automatic paperworkStart here

What the software does, so nobody does the work twice.

See what it doesClose

Other hospitals saved 6 to 8 hours for each patient. Not yet measured here.

  • Starts the going-home papers when a doctor first mentions it, even on the evening round.
  • Keeps a record of every test, medicine and procedure from the day the patient arrives.
  • Keeps the bill up to date all the time, so it is ready the moment the doctor signs.
  • Works out which unused medicines go back to pharmacy, so nurses don’t count them first.
  • Sends a first bill to the insurance company the evening before.
  • Lets the doctor check and sign on a phone or tablet during the morning round.
Changes on the wardsPart 2

What each team does differently, step by step.

See the changesClose
  • The steps stay the same. They start earlier and run side by side.
  • Staff approve what the software prepares, instead of writing it by hand.
  • Your IT team connects the software to your hospital systems, reports and notes.
  • Pharmacy, diet, housekeeping and transport teams are told exactly when they’re needed.
  • If one team does both discharges and admissions, the two jobs are split or run together.
  • The bed desk writes down when each bed is given out and when the next patient arrives.
People who must agreePart 3

Who has to say yes, and what each is asked to do.

See who and whyClose

Each team is told the software takes work off them. It does not replace them.

  • Doctors: start the going-home papers in the evening, then sign on a phone next morning.
  • Nurses: check what the software writes down from the doctors’ notes.
  • Billing and the insurance desk: check bills that are already made.
  • IT, lab and scan teams: keep reports up to date in the system.
  • Admissions, housekeeping, the dietitian and the ward manager: act when the software tells them.
  • Head of Operations: approves the new order of steps. Without this, the change won’t last.
