What it keeps a record ofRecords

Everything about the patient, from the day they arrive.

See the listClose
  • Why they came in, their symptoms and what the doctors found.
  • Every test done, and what it showed, especially anything unusual.
  • Every procedure or operation, with the doctor’s notes.
  • Every medicine given or changed, and how much is left to return.
  • Every supply used, and what is left over.
  • The doctor’s daily notes, and the reason behind each decision.
What it asks of your staffStaff

To check and approve, instead of writing everything by hand.

See who does whatClose
  • Doctors: say who is going home on the evening round, then sign on a phone in the morning.
  • Nurses: check what the software has written down, instead of counting supplies first.
  • Billing and the insurance desk: check bills that are already made.
  • At first it only helps each person. It takes work away only once everyone trusts it.
What must happen firstNeeded first

Your IT team links the software to your hospital systems.

See why this mattersClose
  • The software can only record what it can read, so it needs your patient records, bills, lab reports and scans.
  • Without this link, nothing above works. It is the first job, before anything else.
  • Lab and scan teams need to put reports in on time, so the records stay complete.

Here’s what the software does in your hospital, from the 7 PM round to the patient going home. Why each piece matters:

  • It starts at the evening round, because your doctors already decide then. Nothing new is asked of them.
  • It keeps the bill up to date all day, so billing stops waiting for the nurses to count supplies.
  • It sends the first bill to insurance overnight, so the morning isn’t spent waiting for approval.
